Principles

Three fixed stars to plan by.

  1. I

    Purpose before product

    A sum of money means little until it is attached to something: freedom from work, a child's education, a home, a gift that outlives you. We name the purpose first and price it in today's dollars.

  2. II

    Time over timing

    The horizon does more work than the decimal points. Starting earlier with less can trace a steeper path than starting later with more, and no one can reliably choose the right month to begin.

  3. III

    Today's dollars, always

    A projected figure decades away flatters everyone. We show both lines side by side, the projected amount and what it would buy today, so inflation is never left out of the conversation.

    Method

    A planning conversation, in four stations.

    1. 01

      Listen

      We begin with what the money is for, who depends on it and which dates cannot move. Products are not discussed.

    2. 02

      Map

      Each purpose becomes an amount and a date in today's dollars. Competing purposes are ranked, out loud, by you.

    3. 03

      Model

      Scenarios are traced side by side with every assumption written down: a cautious line, a central line, a late start.

    4. 04

      Review

      Once a year, purposes are revisited before figures. Lives change, so the plan is expected to change with them.

    A plan is a set of assumptions you agree to revisit, written down so that you can.

    The ledger

    Every word on this page, defined.

    Finance hides behind vocabulary. These are the terms the planner uses, in plain English. Each has its own link, so the "What is this?" notes above open the right entry.

    CCompounding

    Growth earned on earlier growth. In the planner, a hypothetical annual rate is converted into an equivalent monthly rate and applied every month, so the curve steepens over time.

    CContribution

    Money you add: a starting amount once, then a monthly amount. Contributions are the straight hatched band at the base of the chart.

    HHorizon

    The number of years between today and the date a purpose needs its money. It is usually the most powerful assumption in the model.

    HHypothetical return

    An assumed constant yearly growth rate used for illustration only. Real returns vary from year to year, can be negative and are never guaranteed. The planner's range of 0–10% is for exploring shapes, not for predicting results.

    IInflation

    The general rise in prices over time. The planner assumes a constant yearly rate and uses it to convert projected amounts into today's dollars.

    PProjected (nominal) value

    The amount the model shows at a future date, before adjusting for inflation. It is the upper brass line.

    PPurpose target

    What a purpose would cost if you paid for it today. Because it is in today's dollars, the planner compares it with the "today's dollars" line, not the projected one.

    SSequence risk

    The risk that poor years arrive at an unlucky moment, such as just before you need the money. A constant-rate model like this one cannot show it, which is one reason it is only an illustration.

    TToday's dollars (real value)

    A future amount expressed in what it would buy now. The platinum line on the chart shows this value.

    VVolatility

    How much values rise and fall along the way. Real paths zigzag. The planner draws smooth curves on purpose, to keep attention on time, contributions and inflation.

    A private conversation

    Begin with a purpose, not a portfolio.

    Tell us what you are planning for. In this concept, a first conversation lasts forty-five minutes, involves no products, and ends with your own assumptions written down for you to keep.

    1. iYou describe the purpose and the dates that matter.
    2. iiWe trace two or three scenarios with you, in today's dollars.
    3. iiiYou leave with a one-page summary of the assumptions.
